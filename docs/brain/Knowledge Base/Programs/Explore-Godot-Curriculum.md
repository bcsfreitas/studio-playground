## Session 1 -- Why do we play games? Why do we mod them?

**Session length:** 60 minutes

**Driving question:** Why do we play games? Why do we mod them?

### Opening Question -- How have games changed over time? (5 min)

Think about the first video game you ever played. Compare it to games today.

- How have graphics, actions or mechanics, and stories changed?
- How have the tools for making games changed?

Read this History of Video Games article to learn more about how video games have evolved.

**Prompt on slide:** How have games changed over time?

### Go Retro! (10 min)

1. Download & Run Godot (instructions).
2. Watch Installing a Game in Godot.
3. Download the Moddable Pong project.
4. Open Godot and load the project.
5. Optional: Adjust display & font size (instructions).
6. Play Pong!

Left paddle = W / S keys,
Right paddle = Up / Down arrow keys

### What Makes it a Game? (5 min)

Watch the Game Elements video to learn about widely-accepted basic game elements.

Designers may group these elements differently, but having a common framework supports systems thinking. Changing one part of a game often affects other parts, too.

**Discussion prompt:** What are the core game elements in Pong? How would you describe its goal, rules, mechanics, space, components, and challenge?

### Make a Mod (25 min)

Mod Pong to make it fresh or more challenging/fun.

For inspiration, watch:

- How Might the Engine Help You Mod the Game?
- Want to Go Further with Your Mods?
- How to Add a Soundtrack to Your Pong Mod
- How to Add Images to Your Pong Mod

Additional Resources:

- Remove.bg (image editing tool)
- Incompetech (royalty free music)
- Freesound.org (sound effects)
- The Melody Loom (app to create your own SFX)

### Share Your Pong Mod (10 min)

Document your changes and post them:

- Record and submit a short video or take screenshots.
- Write down what you changed and why.
- Note how it changed the player experience.
- Give feedback to others who have shared their mods, too!

### Closing Question (5 min)

What could be a great mod for Pong that would change the whole player experience? Dream big!

Watch What is Blue Sky Brainstorming? and imagine you have no tech or skill limits - what would be a groundbreaking mod?

Write it down in your game design journal.

### Self-Check

- I've opened and navigated Godot's UI.
- I've installed and played the moddable Pong game.
- I understand the basics of game elements.
- I've made at least one mod and documented it.
- I've reflected on future ideas.

## Session 2 -- Should game makers also be game players?

**Session length:** 60 minutes

**Driving question:** Should game makers also be game players?

### Opening Question -- What is your favorite platformer? (5 min)

Think of a platformer game you like (e.g., Super Mario Bros, Sonic, Hollow Knight, Ori and the Blind Forest).

- Why do you like it?
- What makes platformers appealing to players in general?

**Prompt on slide:** What is your favorite platformer?

### Let's Play Platformers! (10 min)

Watch the Game Design Journal video, then play one of these free 2D platformer games and record your observations:

- Pikwip
- Kiko Adventure
- Climbable Arrow
- Jumping Shell

### Core Gameplay Loop (5 min)

Watch Core Gameplay Loops.

Games should include an action, followed by a reward, followed by expansion. This is what keeps players coming back!

- Can you identify the core gameplay loop (Action -> Reward -> Expansion) in the platformer you just played?

### Your New Playground! (10 min)

Download and play the Moddable Platformer.

- How does it compare to the other platformers you played?
- Can you think of 2 or 3 possible mods you might want to make?

Resources:

- Godot Tutorial: Installing A Game | From GitHub To Godot

### Mod the Platformer (20 min)

Use what you remember from modding Pong to explore this bigger game.

- Watch How Can I Mod the Platformer?
- Start experimenting with anything that looks interesting in the scene tree, file system and the inspector.

Making small changes and press play often. This will help you build bigger ideas later.

Additional resources: Scenes & Nodes Explained, Adding Your Own Soundtrack to the Platformer

### Share Your Platformer Mod (5 min)

Document your changes and post them:

- Record and submit a short video or take screenshots.
- Write down what you changed and why.
- Note how it changed the player experience.
- Make sure to give feedback to others who have shared their mods, too!

### Closing Question (5 min)

If you made your own platformer game, how would you want players to feel while playing it?

What things could you create to make them feel that way?

Think about the emotional impact of your changes - excitement, tension, frustration, curiosity, etc.

### Self-Check

- I've played at least two platformers and recorded my reflections as a player.
- I understand what a core gameplay loop is.
- I've installed and played the platformer in Godot.
- I've made mods to the platformer.
- I've shared my mods.

## Session 3 -- How do game makers create a fun and engaging level?

**Session length:** 60 minutes

**Driving question:** How do game makers create a fun and engaging level?

### Opening Question -- Is frustration ever a good thing in a game? (5 min)

Games challenge players in different ways. Before going further, take a moment to think about this question:

- Is frustration ever a good thing in a game?

**Prompt on slide:** Is frustration ever a good thing in a game?

### Grayboxing Mindset (5 min)

Grayboxing means building a rough version of a level to test how jumps, enemies, and flow feel together before customizing visuals or details.

- Use existing assets to make fast changes and see how they affect play. Test the flow from start to finish.
- Optional: give yourself one constraint (ex: a player time limit or a fixed level size).

This level is a fast prototype. What you learn here will make your next level stronger.

### How Should This Level Play? (5 min)

You're changing the whole level in the platformer. Watch the How challenging should my level be? video. Consider:

- Who will your level be for? (beginners, experienced players, or both)
- Should players feel relaxed, tense, or somewhere in-between?
- How will you introduce difficulty gradually?

### Improve the Level (30 min)

A level already exists in this game, but it's very basic.

Your goal: make the level more engaging using the existing game components. Remix and extend the level to create something playable from start to finish.

Key Difference: Let's move from quick, playful tinkering to focusing on how the entire level feels to play.

Revisit this resource for support:

- How Can I Mod the Platformer?

### Share Your Practice Level (10 min)

Document your changes and post them:

- Record and submit a short video or take screenshots.
- Write down what you changed and why.
- Note how it changed the player experience.
- Make sure to give feedback to others who have shared their mods, too!

### Closing Question (5 min)

What would you want more time to improve next?

### Self-Check

- I reflected on how challenging my level could be.
- I understand the concept of grayboxing in game design.
- I recreated the base level of the platformer in Godot.
- I playtested my level and reflected on the player experience.

## Session 4 -- What helps an idea move from concept to something real?

**Session length:** 60 minutes

**Driving question:** What helps an idea move from concept to something real?

### Opening Question -- How do you decide what matters most to build? (5 min)

Consider the challenges game makers face: technical (bugs, performance), creative (story, mechanics), player-related (accessibility, balance), etc.

- How do you decide what matters most to build?

**Prompt on slide:** How do you decide what matters most to build?

### Problem Solving in Game Design (5 min)

Watch Breaking down big challenges.

Game components feel like single ideas, but they are built by combining smaller tasks. As you watch, think of a game idea or mod you might want to try and implement.

- What smaller challenges or sub tasks would you need to consider to get it made?

Next up, you'll try breaking your own idea into smaller steps.

### Break it Down! (10 min)

Use tldraw (or paper & pen) to break down a game idea you have.

- Start with one feature or change: a moving platform, a new ability, or a new jump challenge.
- Break it into smaller tasks: controls or input, what it interacts with, and animations or sound effects. Then break those down further, as needed.

This practice is to help you understand the scope or size of your idea, not to limit it. Consider this while planning your level next.

### Problem Decomp (5 min)

Share one game element or mod that you plan to create in the game:

- Share a screenshot of one feature broken down into small tasks.
- Ask for feedback about the decomposition of that task.
- Make sure to give feedback to others who have shared drafts of their breakdown process, too!

### Make an LDD (20 min)

The same way you broke one idea into parts, you'll now plan how all the parts of your level fit together. Game makers use a Level Design Document (LDD) to plan what they will need to create levels in a game.

Use this LDD template to help guide your thinking. Make a copy and start outlining your level.

Do your best to fill this out now: you'll revisit and adjust it as you build and playtest.

### Play Like a Designer (10 min)

As you work on your Level Design Document, keep the game open and replay parts of the platformer with a critical eye.

Look for elements you could reuse: mechanics, patterns, or ideas that fit the game's tone and theme.

Think about what new ideas you could add while staying true to the game's overall feel, not its exact art style.

Write ideas down as you play.

### Closing Question (5 min)

How do you keep the game's overall vibe while also making it your own?

Can you change or mod the game while still ensuring your level feels like it "belongs" in this world of *Threadbare*?

Write down your thoughts in your LDD.

### Self-Check

- I understand how to break a big problem into smaller, solvable parts.
- I've practiced problem decomposition in a game design scenario.
- I filled out my Level Design Document with my plan for my new level.
- I reflected on how my new ideas can feel like they fit with an existing game world
