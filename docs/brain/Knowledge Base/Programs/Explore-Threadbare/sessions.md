## Explore: Threadbare | Core Gameplay & Mechanics

**Discipline:** Game Design

**Session length:** 60 minutes

**Driving question:** What makes the player return to a game over and over again?

### What is Open Source?

*Threadbare* is an open-source game. Its code, art, and assets are free for anyone to use, remix, and improve.

During this session, you'll make your own contribution to the *Threadbare* world:

a core loop for a platformer game

Your contribution will become part of the game and the community we're building!

### Have fun, and... (15 min)

Play Stella and the Missing Star in *Threadbare*!

As you play, try to identify the actions that Stella has to do in each mini-game.

Write down these actions.

### Mechanics & Core Loops (5 min)

Watch the video. Reflect:

- What mechanics are present in Stella and The Missing Star?
- Can you identify the cycle of ACTIONS-REWARD-EXPANSION in that quest?

Write down your conclusions.

### Storyboards in Video Games (5 min)

Watch the video. Reflect:

- What makes a video game storyboard different from a movie or comic storyboard?

Write down your conclusions.

### How would you do it? (10 min)

If *Threadbare* was a platformer:

In what ways would it be different from Stella and The Missing Star? Why?

Then:

- Choose one of the characters on this slide.
- You'll make them the protagonist of a *Threadbare* platformer.

Note: the questions below the names can be applied to any character. Use them as a compass: try to answer them.

- Musician: How would he move?
- Storyvore: What would they like to collect?
- Elder: Could he be a protagonist? Why?
- InkDrinker: Where would they like to go next?

_[image-only slide element: character portraits for the Musician, Storyvore, Elder, and InkDrinker accompany these prompts -- learners choose one based on name and appearance]_

### Make a Storyboard! (20 min)

Practice your game design abilities with the character you chose in the previous slide:

- Determine what action(s), reward(s) and what expansion would work best for them, according to their names and how they look like. Let that inspire you.
- Now, use this storyboard template (or one that suits you best -- you can also use pen and paper) to show the core loop that you created for the character.

### Share your core gameplay loop -- Time to jump in! (5 min)

Use this guide. Post your storyboard and answer:

- What does the character do?
- What makes your core loop interesting or meaningful for the player?

Give feedback to others, too. Use the Compliment, Coach, Encourage method!

- Compliment: "What I like about your work is..."
- Coach: "I think you could improve your work by..."
- Encourage: "I see a lot of potential for..."

### Self-Check

- I analyzed the mechanics on a StoryQuest.
- I know what a core gameplay loop is, and how to identify them in video games.
- I know what a storyboard is, and what makes them particular in video games.
- I created a core gameplay loop for a character.
- I depicted my core loop in a storyboard.

Answer self-check questions here: https://tinyurl.com/ETB-mechanics

### In the industry...

A Gameplay Designer shapes the rules, abilities, and challenges that define how a game plays.

- Designs mechanics, abilities, challenges, and core interactions.
- Prototypes gameplay ideas and refines them through testing and iteration.
- Adjusts pacing, difficulty, and balance to support the player experience.
- Often works with programmers and artists as ideas move from concept to play.

### In other industries...

An Experience Designer creates spaces to explore, like museums or theme parks.

- Designs interactive elements, like buttons, levers, or puzzles, that invite people to try things themselves.
- Tests exhibits with real visitors, watching what draws them in and what people skip.
- Builds in layers of discovery, so there's always something new to notice or try.
- Works with scientists, educators, and engineers to make sure the experience is accurate, safe, and easy to understand.

### Deliverable

A core gameplay loop (actions, reward, expansion) designed for a chosen *Threadbare* character, depicted in a storyboard and shared to the community platform.

### In the industry...

A UX Designer or Interaction Designer makes games intuitive, readable, and satisfying to control.

- Often designs menus, HUDs, tutorials, and interface flows.
- Improves clarity through visuals, feedback, and accessible design choices.
- Refines controls and interactions using player feedback and testing.
- Often works with design, engineering, and art teams to improve how players understand and interact with the game.

### In other industries...

An Industrial Designer creates everyday objects that are easy to use without instructions.

- Shapes buttons, dials, and screens so people instinctively know what to press or turn.
- Adds cues, like a raised bump or a colored light, to guide what to do next.
- Tests prototypes with real people, watching where they get confused or make mistakes.
- Works with engineers and manufacturers to make sure the design can actually be built and work as planned.

### In the industry...

A Progression Designer shapes how players grow, unlock content, and stay engaged over time.

- Designs when and how players gain new abilities, tools, or options.
- Decides what actions earn rewards, and what those rewards are.
- Plans the order and pacing of challenges so players don't get overwhelmed or bored.
- Often works with designers, engineers, and UX teams to test and adjust progression based on how players actually play.

### In other industries...

A Learning Experience Designer shapes how people build skills and stay motivated over time.

- Designs the order and pacing of lessons, tasks, or milestones.
- Decides when learners should unlock new tools, challenges, or levels of difficulty.
- Uses rewards, feedback, and visible progress to keep people motivated.
- Collaborates with educators, product teams, and researchers to improve the learning journey.
